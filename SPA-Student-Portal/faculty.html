Teaching

Academic departments

Every learning area, with the subjects it handles.

How the faculty is organised

Teachers belong to the learning area they teach and are led by a department head, who reports to the Academic Coordinator. Class advisers are assigned per section and are the first point of contact for parents.

Who to approach

  1. Subject or grade concern — the subject teacher first.
  2. Section-level concern — the class adviser.
  3. Curriculum or load concern — the department head, then the Academic Coordinator.

Tip: visit during consultation hours, or send an email to the department so a reply can be logged.

Non-teaching

School offices

The staff who handle records, money, and student support.

What each office does

Non-teaching personnel are grouped by function rather than by subject. Each office keeps its own records, so a concern is settled fastest when it goes straight to the office that owns it.

Office contact guide

  1. Records, certifications and Form 137 — Registrar's Office.
  2. Fees, receipts and balances — Accounting Office.
  3. Counseling and student welfare — Guidance Office.
  4. Accounts and portal problems — IT Support.

Staff access

Staff sign-in

Separate from student accounts — staff records are confidential.

Why staff sign in separately

Student records, grades, and balances are private. Staff accounts carry teacher-level or office-level permissions, so the staff sign-in stays apart from the Student Portal that students and parents use.

Getting an account

  1. Ask the school office to create your staff account.
  2. Sign in and change the temporary password you were given.
  3. Report a lost password to the IT Support office for a reset.

Note: never share a staff account. Every change made in the dashboard is recorded against the account that made it.
